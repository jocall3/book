# Chapter 6: Prose and Poetry: Why Your Writing Sucks (and How to Fix It)

Let's face it. You've poured your heart into crafting the "perfect" scene. You've toiled over every word, every comma, every metaphor. You hit publish, brimming with anticipation. Then...crickets. Ten views. Maybe a pity clap from your mom.

Sound familiar?

The engine of any story is its plot, the chassis its characters. But the prose—the very words on the page—is the road itself. And right now, your road is paved with potholes, detours, and confusing signage.

Forget the "Clarity Wars." This isn't about choosing sides; it's about writing prose that *people actually want to read.*

## The Brutal Truth: Why No One's Reading Your Masterpiece

Before we dive into solutions, let's dissect the problem. Here's why your current prose is probably failing:

*   **It's Boring:** Let's be honest. Is your writing *exciting*? Does it grab the reader by the throat and refuse to let go? Or does it gently lull them to sleep?
*   **It's Too Damn Complicated:** You're not writing a doctoral thesis. Stop trying to impress people with your vocabulary and start connecting with them on a human level.
*   **It's All "Tell," No "Show":** We get it. You can describe a sunset. But can you make us *feel* the warmth on our skin, the salty air on our faces, the pang of longing in our hearts?
*   **It's Self-Indulgent:** Are you writing for *your* enjoyment, or for the *reader's* benefit? If you're not solving a problem, sparking curiosity, or offering genuine value, why should anyone bother reading?

## The Viral Formula: A No-BS Guide to Prose That Pops

Forget the fancy theories. Here's a practical, actionable plan to transform your prose from drab to dynamic:

**1. Embrace the Semi-Controversial:**

Don't be a pushover. Take a stance. Challenge conventional wisdom. Offer a fresh perspective.

**Example:** Instead of writing a generic post about "writing tips," write "Why 'Show, Don't Tell' is Ruining Modern Fiction."

The key is to be *semi*-controversial. Don't pick fights just for the sake of it. Make a point, back it up with reasoning, and be prepared for pushback.

**2. Cut the Crap:**

Seriously. Prune every unnecessary word, phrase, and sentence. Ruthlessly eliminate:

*   **Long, Rambling Sentences:** Keep it short. Keep it punchy.
*   **Jargon and Obscure Vocabulary:** Write like you're talking to a friend, not a professor.
*   **Redundant Descriptions:** If it doesn't add value, it's gone.
*   **Excessive Adverbs:** "He said sadly" is almost always weaker than "He sighed."

**3. Master the "Hook":**

Your opening sentence is your only chance to grab the reader's attention. Make it count.

**Bad Hook:** "In this chapter, we will explore the intricacies of prose and poetry."

**Good Hook:** "Let's face it. Your writing sucks."

**4. Show, Don't Just Tell (But Know When to Tell):**

Yes, "show, don't tell" is important. But it's not a hard-and-fast rule. Sometimes, you need to cut to the chase and simply *tell* the reader what's happening.

**Example:** Instead of spending three paragraphs describing a character's internal struggle, simply write: "He was conflicted." Then, *show* the consequences of that conflict through his actions.

**5. Inject Your Voice:**

Don't be afraid to let your personality shine through. Be authentic. Be vulnerable. Be yourself.

**Example:** Notice how *I'm* not afraid to swear and be brutally honest in this very chapter? That's my voice. Find yours.

**6. Prioritize the Reader's Experience:**

Always ask yourself: "What's in it for the reader?" Are you entertaining them? Informing them? Inspiring them? Solving a problem for them?

If you can't answer that question, rewrite the damn thing.

## The Luminous Pane: It's About Balance, Stupid

Forget the "Stylistic Modulation" mumbo jumbo. It boils down to this:

*   **Fast-Paced Scenes:** Use short, punchy sentences and simple language. Keep the momentum going.
*   **Reflective Moments:** Allow yourself to be more lyrical and descriptive. But don't get carried away.

Consider the simple act of a character entering a dark room:

*   **The Old You:** He opened the door and stepped inside. The room was dark. He couldn't see a thing. Fear pricked his skin. (Boring!)
*   **The "Artisan" You:** The threshold beckoned, a geometric maw of absolute negation, and he surrendered his form to its silent, suffocating embrace, where the very concept of light was but a forgotten rumor whispered by dying gods. (Pretentious!)
*   **The New, Viral-Ready You:** He shoved the door open. Darkness slammed into him like a fist. He couldn't see a damn thing, but the fear was immediate and primal.

## Final Thought: Stop Overthinking It

Writing viral prose isn't rocket science. It's about being authentic, being engaging, and being relentlessly focused on the reader's experience.

So, stop agonizing over every word and start writing something that actually connects with people.

And for God's sake, get a better hook.
