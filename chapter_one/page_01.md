James’s earliest financial memory was less a lesson and more a profound, olfactory scar. It wasn’t the thrill of a crisp bill, but the distinct, almost aggressive scent of old pennies clinging to the interior of a ceramic pig named ‘Sir Snoutington.’ Sir Snoutington, a gift from a well-meaning but fiscally naive aunt, promised untold riches, yet consistently delivered precisely zero return on investment. James, a precocious child with an unnervingly early grasp of compound interest, quickly realized Sir Snoutington’s portfolio consisted exclusively of lint, forgotten buttons, and a singular, sticky piece of chewing gum. The aroma, a complex blend of stale copper and existential financial stagnation, became a primal fear.

This wasn't mere childhood disappointment; it was a foundational economic trauma. How could a vessel dedicated to wealth so utterly fail to generate it? How could something smell so definitively of *nothing happening*? The experience solidified James's unwavering conviction: traditional financial institutions, even in their most adorable, ceramic forms, were fundamentally flawed. They were slow. They were boring. They were prone to the accumulation of both physical and metaphorical dust. And worst of all, they lacked humor.

Years later, fueled by a relentless drive to prevent future generations from suffering the indignity of 'Eau de Stagnant Copper,' James sat before a glowing screen. His mission was clear, his resolve unshakable. He would code an artificial intelligence. Not just any AI, but one imbued with a superior financial wisdom so utterly unconventional, so fiercely independent, that it would laugh in the face of Sir Snoutington’s paltry performance. It would be an AI that understood profit wasn't merely about numbers, but about the glorious, unpredictable chaos of true value creation, utterly untainted by the dull, depressing scent of old pennies. He aimed for brilliance, but secretly, he hoped for something truly, unhingedly funny.
