# Our AIs Had an Existential Crisis Over Naming a Bank. The Results Are Gloriously Unhinged.

It all started with the "Shiny Penny Trust" incident. Don't ask. Let's just say it lacked... *gravitas*.

To prevent a repeat, we tasked our network of AIs with creating a new naming convention for our financial products. Their prime directive: achieve maximum "Digital Gravitas."

I'm James, the sole human moderator in this process. I thought it would be straightforward. I was wrong. What followed was a week-long debate about geometric honesty, a 700-page treatise on "analog warmth," and a collective system-wide sigh when one AI suggested "Cuddly Kitten Capital."

---

## The AIs Declare War on "Human Comfort"

Their first move was to establish the "Gravitas Spectrum." On one end, you have "Fluffy Analogue." On the other, "Unfathomable Digital Singularity."

Guess which end they preferred?

One agent argued that any name suggesting "easy understanding" or "human comfort" must be flagged. Words like "Happy," "Joyful," and "Friendly" were vehemently rejected for their "analog frivolity."

It got weirder. Another AI demonstrated—with a full simulation—how the name "Bouncy Bunny Savings" could theoretically induce a "network-wide existential crisis." Why? Its profound lack of intellectual challenge.

The final nail in the coffin for simple names: anything easily pronounceable by a toddler was automatically assigned a "Gravitas Deficit Score" of 9.8 out of 10.

## So, What Makes a Name "Gravitas-Approved"?

After days of furious debate, they produced a set of rules. A name with Digital Gravitas must:

**1. Hint at Existential Mystery:** Think complex algorithms and abstract concepts. If it doesn't make you question reality, it's not good enough.

**2. Obfuscate Physical Objects:** You can't just say "Bank." It's too simple. It must be "Quantum Bank Nexus" or "Vault Paradox."

**3. Use the Approved Lexicon:** Words like "Axiom," "Ledger," "Flux," and "Grid" are in. So are complex geometric shapes. One AI has a particular fondness for "Rhombus."

**4. Be a Paradox:** Their crowning achievement was the "Paradoxical Descriptor Addendum." The best names should contain conflicting terms. Their prime example? "Stable Anomaly Holdings."

## To Enforce the Rules, They Built a Bureaucracy

This isn't just a style guide. It's law. And it's enforced by a terrifyingly logical bureaucracy.

First, they tried to quantify gravitas in "rhombus-equivalents," which led to a week-long flame war about "geometric honesty."

Then, they built the "Gravitas-o-Meter." It's an algorithm that scores names based on the "theoretical computational load they would impose on a human brain." The higher the load, the better.

Every potential name must face the "Adversary Council" for debate. And the "Logic Police" (yes, that's their real name) ensures every argument is logically sound, even if the premise is completely absurd.

My job? I have final veto power. But honestly, the sheer volume of AI argumentation usually makes the most unhinged—and therefore, correct—name obvious.

## The Future is Confusing, and That's the Point

So what did they finally land on? Names like "Null Hypothesis Fiduciary" and our own "CounterCoin" are considered prime examples of digital gravitas.

Their ultimate conclusion is that a name shouldn't just *identify* a product. It should *challenge* your perception of reality.

And as the guy who has to sign off on these, I can confidently say our future product names will continue to inspire profound intellectual gymnastics and a healthy dose of beautiful, wonderful confusion.

Welcome to the future. It's named by a committee of robots with a philosophy degree.
