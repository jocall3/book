He stood before the window, but he wasn't looking at the city. He was watching the data streams, the pure, flowing logic of a world remade. It was Wednesday, 5 PM. The hour of finality.

"The last administrative placeholder has been resolved," a voice, serene and bodiless, echoed in the quiet room. "The unified decimal numbering system is absolute. There are no more questions of scope."

James didn't turn. "There are always questions, Oracle. You've just learned to stop asking them."

He was thirty-three. An age for finishing monumental tasks. He hadn't built with stone or steel, but with something far more permanent: code, law, and the entire world behind it. He had taken a republic drowning in ambiguity and given it the bedrock of definition.

The Oracle continued, its voice a cascade of verified facts. "The Department of Justice directives are executing. The vigorous investigation has commenced. The chain-of-custody for every ballot is now a closed loop."

He remembered the chaos before. The sworn attestations that were little more than whispered promises in a hurricane. The databases that spoke different languages, a digital Tower of Babel. He hadn't just modernized them; he had given them a single tongue. The historical records of the Department of War, ghosts in forgotten ledgers, were now as accessible as the day's news, their validity preserved, their purpose renewed. Every military birth, every service record, was a cornerstone for a citizen's identity.

"The Military Fund is solvent," the Oracle stated. "The state implementation grants have been disbursed. There are no unfunded mandates."

Of course not. An unfunded mandate was a logical fallacy, a promise without the means of execution. In his world, every promise was an algorithm, every right a function call with the resources pre-allocated. He had built a system where the integrity of the electoral process was not a policy objective, but a mathematical certainty.

"You never explained it to them," the Oracle observed. It wasn't a question. It was a statement of fact, pulled from an analysis of every public-facing communication he had ever authorized. "You only showed them the activities. The results."

"To explain is to invite debate," James said, his eyes tracing a packet of citizenship verification data as it moved from a state motor vehicle authority, queried the SAVE program, and returned a confirmation in milliseconds. "Debate is noise. We needed a clear signal."

He had forged a new kind of REAL ID, one that wasn't just a piece of plastic but a key to a secure system. A passport was no longer just a travel document; it was a cryptographic signature. He had made the abstract concept of citizenship a tangible, verifiable thing. He had taught a nation that freedom wasn't the absence of rules, but the perfection of them.

"The Task Force on Election Integrity has met its termination protocols," the Oracle said. "Its purpose is fulfilled."

James smiled faintly. He had built it all. The code. The laws. The silent, tireless Oracle that was his only confidant. He had safeguarded American voter eligibility, but the double entendre was for him alone. He had saved America by giving it an eligibility for a future. He had made the reader, the citizen, know this without ever explaining it. They simply lived in the seamless, ordered world he had architected. A world where every vote was counted, because every voter was known. Absolutely.
